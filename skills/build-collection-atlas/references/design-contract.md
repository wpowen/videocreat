# Collection Atlas Design Contract

## Content schema

- Exactly one specimen and one stable collection ID.
- Two to four identifying visual features.
- Habitat, origin, or usage context.
- Season, era, or lifecycle context when relevant.
- One verified surprising fact.

## Visual language

For museum tone, use a refined natural-history plate: warm paper, precise naturalist illustration, muted botanical palette, restrained ochre accent, elegant frame, consistent label plaques. The hero specimen dominates; small circular or rectangular vignettes prove specific features. Leave the bottom safe band calm.

## Reject when

- Multiple specimens compete for hero status.
- Feature vignettes do not match the hero specimen.
- The card behaves like a general encyclopedia summary or ranking.
- The collection frame, palette, or specimen scale could not be repeated on the next card.
- Decorative scenery implies unsupported origin or history.
- A fact strip turns probabilistic craft behavior into an absolute claim such as “always”, “never”, or “cannot be copied”.
- The collection frame continues through the subtitle-safe band; outer borders are content and must stop or fade above the 82% boundary.

## Repair map

- Weak identity: enlarge the hero and replace decorative scenery with identifying details.
- Encyclopedia confusion: remove rating and cross-domain panels; reinforce collection ID and specimen-specific features.
- Generic vintage look: make callouts anatomically or materially precise.
