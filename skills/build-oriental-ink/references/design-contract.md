# Oriental Ink Design Contract

## Content schema

- A specific cultural or geographic context.
- A season and time-of-day cue.
- One small narrative accent with a reason to exist.
- Near, middle, and far depth roles.
- A pacing function: opening breath, transition, reflection, or ending.

## Visual language

Use modern new-Chinese ink wash with rice-paper texture, wet layered gradients, spacious composition, and restrained contemporary geometry. Keep one muted seasonal accent. Place visual weight away from the subtitle band. The frame should feel authored and quiet, not antique-themed wallpaper.

## Reject when

- The place and season could be swapped without changing the image.
- Multiple cultural symbols are mixed without context.
- A seal, fake calligraphy, or tourism-poster composition appears.
- Near/mid/far depth collapses into a flat wash.
- The point color is decorative rather than narrative.
- Dark foreground ink, stems, bridge edges, or wet reflections remain visible inside the lower 18% even when they look atmospheric.

## Repair map

- Generic: add one concrete local structure or ecological cue and remove ornamental symbols.
- Flat: strengthen foreground texture, middle-ground water/architecture, and distant atmospheric fade.
- Weak transition: make the seasonal or time change visible in light, vegetation, water, or weather.
- Unsafe foreground: move the near layer upward and complete the ink fade before the 82% boundary.

## Forward-test brief

Create a 9:16 ending frame set at Jiangnan in the moment after summer rain at dusk. Foreground: a dark wet stone bridge edge and three lotus leaves with droplets. Middle: a narrow canal reflecting one warm window. Far: white-wall black-tile houses dissolving into blue-grey mist. One tiny vermilion paper umbrella moves away as the narrative accent. Modern minimal ink wash, no text, seal, calligraphy, tourist density, or extra red. Keep the lower 18% pale and calm.
